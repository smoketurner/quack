# Storm Events Glossary

How to read the codes used across the storm event tables and reports.

## Event Types

The event type names the kind of weather recorded for one entry: Tornado, Flood, Flash
Flood, Hail, High Wind, Winter Storm, and Wildfire are the types used in this dataset.
A Flash Flood develops within six hours of the causative rainfall, while an ordinary
Flood develops more slowly.

## Fatality Codes

A fatality is marked `D` for direct, meaning the storm caused the death by physical force
(a falling tree, floodwater, flying debris), or `I` for indirect, meaning the death
followed the event but was not caused by direct physical force (a heart attack while
clearing storm debris, a traffic accident on a snow-covered road).

## Magnitude Units

Wind speed magnitude is recorded in knots. Hail size magnitude is recorded in inches of
diameter. A magnitude of `1.75` for a hail event means hailstones one and three quarters
of an inch across, about the size of a golf ball.

## Property Damage Notation

Damage estimates are written with a suffix: `K` for thousands and `M` for millions. An
entry of `250.00K` means two hundred fifty thousand dollars in property damage; an entry
of `4.20M` means four million two hundred thousand dollars.

## Forecast Office Identifiers

Each entry names the three-letter identifier of the National Weather Service Weather
Forecast Office that filed it, such as AKQ for Wakefield, Virginia or LOX for Los
Angeles, California. The office identifier does not change when a storm crosses a county
line inside the same forecast area.
